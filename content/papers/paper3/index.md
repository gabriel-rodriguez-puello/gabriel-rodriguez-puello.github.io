##### Abstract

This paper examines how local economic shocks affect entrepreneurship, considering both the entry into entrepreneurship and the survival and performance of existing firms. Exploiting Sweden's 2004 mining boom as an exogenous shock, we use administrative data (2000–2015) and difference-in-differences estimates comparing individuals within 80 km of a mine to those 80–150 km away. The boom increased entrepreneurial entry but not the number of new firms, as many entrants were hybrid entrepreneurs reallocating effort to existing ventures. Incumbents gained through higher capital income, while treated firms expanded employment and wage costs without improving operating profits.
